Tsaatan Reindeer Expedition

The Tsaatan, also called Dukha, keep small reindeer herds in the taiga of northern Khuvsgul. A winter visit is a 7, 8, or 9 day expedition, not a day trip from the capital. Most groups fly north and then drive toward the community. A teepee stay and a reindeer ride happen only when that family and that herd can take guests. Snow and a closed track can add a day, which is why the longer plan exists. Read the day-by-day options on the Tsaatan reindeer expedition page.

Gobi Camel Festival

Umnugovi’s camel festival is the reason many winter travelers go south: two-humped Bactrian camels, races, riding, and performances around herder families. The public calendar on this site does not lock a day and month. The tour is planned as 4 or 5 days inside the November–April season, and the exact festival days are written into the quote once they are confirmed. Start with the Gobi Camel Festival tour.

Terelj Dog Sledding Tour

Gorkhi-Terelj National Park is close enough to Ulaanbaatar for a single day. Pickup is from the hotel, the drive is through the winter valley, and the sled run happens when the snow and the kennel allow it. There is time for photographs and an optional lunch in a ger before the return. It is the practical add-on before a flight home, or the first winter day after landing. Details are on the Terelj dog sledding page.

Khuvsgul Ice Festival

Khuvsgul is Mongolia’s large northern lake. In winter the shore freezes and the ice festival, when it is held, uses that ice. The tour is 5 to 7 days including the flight to Murun and the drive to the lake. Horse sledding is listed only where a local operator is actually running it that week. We do not publish a fixed festival date here. The Khuvsgul Ice Festival tour is the page to request the current week.

Western Mongolia Eagle Hunter Experience

Bayan-Ulgii, in the far west, is a Kazakh region. Eagle hunters train birds through the cold months, and a family visit is an 8 to 10 day trip because of the flight to Ulgii and the valley drives. A hunt is never promised. The days are for the household, the birds, and the winter mountains. See the eagle hunter experience.

Winter Nomadic Family Experience

Not every winter trip needs a flight. Two or three days with a herder family near Terelj or another valley outside Ulaanbaatar shows livestock kept close to the ger, a home meal, and a normal winter evening. It is a household, not a performance. The same area also has a separate heated ger camp stay and a one-day city tour of Gandan, the museums, and Sukhbaatar Square. The family stay itself is the winter nomadic experience.

Winter Horse Riding and Snow Trekking

Horse riding in winter is a 2 or 3 day ride where the ground is not icy and the horses are fit. The night is in a ger. If the track freezes, the ride is shortened. Snow trekking is a guided walk of 1 or 2 days in Terelj or a nearby valley, with the same rule: wind, ice, or a short afternoon stops the route. These are not glacier climbs. Compare the winter horse ride and the snow trek.

Winter Gobi Desert Tours

Outside festival week, the South Gobi is still a winter landscape: Yolyn Am’s canyon, the Flaming Cliffs at Bayanzag, and the Khongoryn Els dunes. A private or small-group expedition takes 5 to 7 days, usually with a flight to Dalanzadgad. Camps that close for the season are left off the route, and the order of the stops moves if the wind closes a track. That itinerary is the Gobi winter expedition, separate from the camel festival tour above.

Mongolia Winter Travel Guide

Ulaanbaatar is the hub. Terelj, a family ger, and the city tour start from a hotel there. Khuvsgul, the Tsaatan taiga, Bayan-Ulgii, and most Gobi routes use a domestic flight plus a road transfer. Winter roads are open, but a storm can hold a group for a day, which is already built into the longer estimates. Daylight is short in December and January, so driving days start early. Gers used for guests are heated. Festival tickets, if a town charges one, and domestic flights are quoted apart from the guide and the vehicle.

Best Time to Visit Mongolia in Winter

The season used for these tours is November through April. November and early December are cold but often clearer. January is the deep cold, especially in the city and the north. February still holds snow for Terelj and the lake. March and April can be windy in the Gobi, and the ice on Khuvsgul is no longer a place to walk once it starts to break. None of those months is published here as the official day of the camel festival or the ice festival. Ask for the week that matches the event you want, and we confirm it before you pay.

What to Wear in Mongolia in Winter

Plan for dry cold, not rain. A windproof shell, a warm mid-layer, and thermal underwear matter more than one heavy coat. Bring boots with a grip for ice, a hat that covers the ears, gloves, a scarf, and sunglasses. The sun on snow is strong even when the air is far below freezing. City days need the same layers because you walk between museums. For a taiga or eagle-hunter trip, pack as if a vehicle wait in the open is possible. The guide will say if a particular camp lends extra felt boots.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are the festival dates fixed on this page?

No. Camel festival and Khuvsgul ice festival days change, and this page does not invent them. The season is November to April. The exact days go on the quote after they are confirmed.

Can I do the dog sled and a longer tour?

Yes. The Terelj dog sled is one day from Ulaanbaatar, so it fits before or after a Gobi, Khuvsgul, or Tsaatan trip.

Is a reindeer ride guaranteed?

No. Riding and a teepee stay depend on the herd and the host family. The expedition still visits the community when the road is open.
